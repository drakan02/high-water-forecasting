## Source verification

- Inspected the complete public GESLA S3 `parquet_files/` catalog: 5,119 objects and 46 GBR BODC-lineage objects. Downloaded and hashed all 46 into ignored local `data/raw/gesla_s3/` (284,854,849 bytes for the 44 paper matches; extras are Felixstowe and Moray Firth). Raw station data are not staged or redistributed.
- Read each matched Parquet schema and values. All 44 Hibbert port numbers map once to a distinct GESLA object; the two extras are retained in inventory. Identity, coordinates, actual time span, cadence, 2006–2010 rows, unit, QC/use flags and SHA-256 are recorded in `reports/network_2010/gesla_bodc_station_inventory.csv` and `data/source_inventory.csv`.
- `unit=m` is preserved. No vertical conversion, interpolation, flag relaxation, or channel combination was used. Original BODC channel, datum linkage, and exact GESLA release are unresolved.
- Port 44's paper name Plymouth is matched to Devonport; official NTSLF lists Plymouth (Devonport), and BODC's network history uses Devonport (Plymouth). Sources: [GESLA downloads/releases](https://gesla787883612.wordpress.com/downloads/), [GESLA format and QC](https://gesla787883612.wordpress.com/format/), [GESLA license](https://gesla787883612.wordpress.com/license/), [Hibbert et al. (2015)](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3179635/1/Hibbert_etal_2015.pdf), [NTSLF UK network](https://ntslf.org/tides/uk-network), [BODC annual network report](https://www.bodc.ac.uk/data/hosted_data_systems/sea_level/uk_tide_gauge_network/reports/documents/2013annualreport.pdf).

## Registered execution and outputs

- UTide 0.4.0 ran for all 44 ports, fitting exact-minute-00 finite/non-fill observations where GESLA `qc_flag=1` and `use_flag=1`. Fit period is `max(1990-01-01, actual source start)` through 2009-12-31. Each station's actual fit count, coordinates, constituent set and state are in its ignored local `transfer_manifest.json`/coefficients; all hourly reconstructions and per-port transfer configs/logs are retained locally.
- ECM OLS ran for 42 ports using all available eligible 2006–2009 training peaks, 24-hour issue lead and immutable N−2/N−4/N−6 lag IDs. Port Ellen (22) and Bournemouth (41) remain in the inventory and UTide output; ECM is `NOT_APPLICABLE_PER_PAPER`, as Hibbert describes multiple highs/lows daily at those locations.
- Peak observation availability is timestamp-based with no provider latency evidence; the configured issue lead and lag availability flags preserve the forecast assumption but do not prove real-time publication latency.
- Also scored a retrospective 2006–2009 model fit on that same interval. Those rows are explicitly in-sample.
- Saved 176 model × port × period rows and 119,220 peak-level records. `reports/network_2010/run_status.csv` contains all ports and actual context/candidate/eligible cycle counts. `station_fit_manifest.csv` records per-port valid fit rows, coordinates, fit period, constituent count/list, and target-cycle counts; `harmonic_coefficients.csv` exports all 2,991 UTide constituent rows; `ecm_models.csv` exports 84 frozen/retrospective coefficient sets, fit sizes/ranks and model hashes. Candidate 2010 intervals range 705–1,256; QC-eligible counts range 13–701. In particular Sheerness has only 13 eligible peaks. Bournemouth and Weymouth have high candidate counts (1,256 and 1,242); their intervals are retained and this cycle interpretation remains unresolved. Decision v2 records this limitation without changing thresholds post hoc.
- Target is total observed H in metres. GESLA has no matched residual-surge term here, and no surge was estimated from H−A. UTide is a substitute for the paper's EHM, not a claim of equivalence.
